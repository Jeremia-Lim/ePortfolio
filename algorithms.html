Algorithms and Data Structures Enhancement

This section of my ePortfolio presents the algorithms and data structures enhancement completed for my Android Inventory Management application. The enhancement demonstrates my ability to evaluate how data is organized, processed, searched, and presented within a software application.

Artifact

Android Inventory Management Application

The original application provided the core functionality necessary to manage inventory items, including adding, editing, deleting, and displaying inventory information. During CS 499, I revisited the application to improve the way inventory data is handled and to demonstrate a stronger understanding of algorithms and data structures.

View Original Artifact View Algorithms Enhancement

Enhancement Narrative

The artifact selected for this enhancement is my Android Inventory Management application, originally developed during CS 360: Mobile Architecture and Programming. I selected this artifact because inventory management relies heavily on the organization and manipulation of data. Users need to be able to retrieve inventory information efficiently and interact with collections of items in a way that is clear and responsive.

The original application demonstrated my ability to create a functional Android application, but revisiting the project gave me the opportunity to consider the efficiency and organization of the underlying data. Earlier in my computer science education, I often focused primarily on whether a feature produced the expected result. Through later coursework, I developed a stronger understanding of why the choice of data structures and algorithms also matters. A solution should not only work correctly but should also be organized, maintainable, and capable of handling data efficiently.

The enhancement allowed me to apply this understanding to the inventory application. Inventory information is naturally represented as a collection of objects. Each inventory item contains related information such as an identifier, item name, and quantity. Organizing this information into structured objects and collections makes the application easier to understand and allows inventory operations to be performed more consistently.

I also considered the algorithms used when displaying and manipulating inventory information. Operations such as searching, sorting, filtering, updating, and deleting items require the application to locate and process specific information within a collection. These operations demonstrate the connection between the data structure selected to hold information and the algorithms used to work with that information.

The enhancement strengthened my understanding of how algorithmic decisions affect the user experience. An inventory application may contain a relatively small amount of information during testing, but a real application could eventually manage much larger collections of data. Considering efficiency during development helps create solutions that can continue to perform effectively as the amount of information increases.

Data Structures and Algorithmic Thinking

One of the most important lessons demonstrated by this enhancement is that data structures should be selected based on the problem being solved. Lists and object collections are useful for representing inventory records because they allow related objects to be stored, retrieved, displayed, and modified. The application can then use algorithms to process these collections according to the user's actions.

My previous coursework also provided experience with structures such as arrays, lists, and trees, including the implementation of a binary search tree. Those experiences helped me understand that different structures provide different advantages depending on how information needs to be accessed. Applying this knowledge to the Inventory Management application helped me evaluate the project from an efficiency perspective rather than considering functionality alone.

Course Outcomes

This enhancement supports the CS 499 course outcome involving the design and evaluation of computing solutions using algorithmic principles and computer science practices. By examining the way inventory information is represented and processed, I demonstrated my ability to consider both correctness and efficiency when developing a software solution.

The enhancement also contributed to my ability to communicate technical decisions. Selecting an algorithm or data structure requires understanding the advantages and limitations of different approaches. Explaining these decisions through the enhancement narrative demonstrates my ability to communicate why a particular solution is appropriate rather than simply presenting working code.

In addition, this work supports the broader goal of developing maintainable software. Clear data models and predictable algorithms make an application easier to understand, test, debug, and modify. These considerations are important when collaborating with other developers because future contributors must be able to understand how information moves through the application.

Reflection

Completing this enhancement changed the way I think about algorithms and data structures in application development. Previously, I often associated these concepts with individual programming exercises. Revisiting the Inventory Management application demonstrated that these concepts are also fundamental to practical software development.

One challenge involved determining where algorithmic improvements would provide meaningful benefits without unnecessarily increasing the complexity of the application. More complicated solutions are not automatically better. Developers must consider the size of the data, the operations being performed, maintainability, and the needs of the user when selecting an approach.

The enhancement reinforced the importance of balancing efficiency with readability and maintainability. An effective computing solution should use appropriate algorithms and data structures while remaining understandable to other developers. This experience strengthened my ability to evaluate technical alternatives and make decisions based on the requirements of the software rather than simply choosing the most complex solution.

Overall, the algorithms and data structures enhancement demonstrates my growth in analyzing how software manages information. I am now more comfortable evaluating collections, data models, searching and sorting operations, and the efficiency of different approaches. These skills will help me develop software that remains organized, responsive, and maintainable as its requirements and data grow.
